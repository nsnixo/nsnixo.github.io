Chapter One

The Freakshow

Where trust begins to unravel

⏳ 6 min read

Miro and I played every day at the same hour. It was the one thing I could count on — the one thread that had held seven years together without fraying.

That day, the thread snapped.

No reply. No ring. Just silence where his voice should have been. I called again. And again. Each attempt felt more desperate than the last, but I didn't care. Miro was my only friend. When you only have one, you hold on tighter than you should.

On the fifth try, he picked up.

"Did you check the group?" His voice was strange — tight, expectant, like he was holding his breath underwater. — Miro, the first crack

"Group? What group?"

"Check the group, man."

A notification buzzed against my ear. Miro added you to The Freakshow.

I stared at the name. The Freakshow. Who names a group that? And why was I only being added now, like an afterthought, like someone who had to be let in through the back door?

I stayed on the call, phone pressed hot against my cheek, and opened the chat.

Two girls were already talking. I recognized Sera immediately — my best friend, my close friend, the one person I thought would never keep secrets from me. The other girl was a stranger. A complete blank. A name I'd never seen, attached to words I didn't understand.

Sera: Go on. Tell him.
Stranger: Sera, we talked about this.
Sera: I helped you. And you're saying we talked about this?

I stepped in. Not because I wanted to, but because the silence on Miro's end of the call was pressing down on me, and someone had to break it.

Wait, I typed. Let me just check what's going on.

The stranger's response came fast: See? I told you he won't like it.

And then Sera, my Sera, said the words that tilted the room: She likes you.

The stranger — the blank name, the unfamiliar profile picture — started to unravel in real time. No, no, I didn't say that! Why are you doing this? I never told you that! She was fighting, panicking, and Sera just let her drown in it.

Then my phone buzzed privately. A DM from Sera.

Sera (DM): I'll explain everything. Just wait.

K, I typed back.

She didn't reply.

I returned to the group. The stranger had stopped fighting. Her next message was quiet, defeated, stripped of all the panic from before:

She's right. I do love you. — A stranger's confession

I read it twice. Then a third time. Love. That word. Thrown at me by someone I had never seen, never heard, never known existed until five minutes ago.

How long has this been going on? I asked.

A month, she said. Sera's been telling me everything about you. We planned this. It was supposed to be a surprise.

A surprise. A month of my best friend feeding pieces of me to a stranger like breadcrumbs, and I hadn't felt a single tug on the line.

How can you love me? I typed. You've never seen me. Never met me.

I just do, she said. I'm just in love, you know?

I didn't know. I didn't know anything about love. It was a foreign language, and everyone in that group seemed fluent except me. — Kael

But Miro was already scheming. He messaged me separately, buzzing with an idea: we should prank her. Send a fake photo — some handsome stranger from Google — and say it was me. A test. A joke. I didn't think much of it. None of this felt real anyway, so what was one more lie stacked on top?

She sent her photo first. Not privately, like she'd promised — but in the group, where everyone could see.

So I sent mine. The fake one. The handsome stranger from a search engine.

Sera (DM): Bro, when did you get plastic surgery?

I didn't reply.

I looked at the girl's photo. She was beautiful. Objectively, undeniably beautiful. But I felt nothing. Just a hollow click of recognition — oh, that's what she looks like — and then emptiness.

That's not you, I said in the group.

She panicked again. Texted Miro separately. That IS me! What did you tell him?

And Miro, the friend I'd trusted for seven years, played along: Ohhh, that's you? I didn't recognize you.

Bro, just accept her proposal. She's pretty. Trust me. Do it.
Why would I?
Bro, trust me. Just do it. — Miro, pushing Kael off the ledge

I was tired. Disoriented. Everyone around me seemed to know the script except me. So I let Miro take the pen.

Fine. How?

Send me a text saying she's pretty. With emojis.

I typed it out — hollow words, borrowed enthusiasm, emojis like little masks — and sent it to him. He forwarded it to her. She accepted.

Just like that, I was in love. My first relationship. A door I never chose to walk through, pushed open by hands that weren't my own.

The first few days were what I imagined relationships were supposed to be. Talking. Sharing hobbies. Fumbling through conversations I wasn't sure I was allowed to have. I didn't know how any of it worked. There was no manual. No guide. Just me, improvising.

But then Miro's texts started shifting.

Miro: Be careful.

No context. No explanation.

Miro (days later): Just... be careful, man.

And again.

Each message was a crack in the foundation I hadn't even known was already broken. — End of Chapter One
